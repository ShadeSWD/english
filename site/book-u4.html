LIFE IN SCIENCEЖизнь в науке: А. Н. Крылов

In 1898, for the first time in the history of the Royal Institution of Naval Architects in Britain, its Gold Medal was awarded* to a foreigner. The foreigner was a Russian scientist who had given a talk about his new theory of ship’s oscillations on waves*. At that time, when constructing a ship, the builders knew little about its future characteristics – how stable* would it be in a storm? how fast could it sail? The new theory answered these questions, and did it so well, that even now, over a hundred years later, naval architects still use it all over the world. The Russian scientist’s name was Alexey Nikolayevich Krylov. He was born in a village by the Volga in 1863. When the family visited Sevastopol, Alexey became very interested in ships and the sea, and in 1878 he went to the Maritime High School in Saint Petersburg. In addition to the School courses he took lessons from the famous mathematician A.M.Lyapunov, the author of stability* theory.

On graduating Krylov went to work at the Main Hydrographic Administration* where he carried out research into magnetic compass deviation*, a topic he often returned to in later years. After that, he joined the department of ship construction of Saint Petersburg Maritime Academy. There he met another brilliant mathematician – A.N.Korkin, who was an expert in differential equations*. Krylov learned much from him, and he graduated First Class in 1 after only two years of study. Korkin then persuaded* Krylov to stay at the Maritime Academy as a mathematics and ship-theory lecturer. In 1900 Krylov got the post of Head of the Naval Administration Tank, which had started functioning six years earlier to carry out model ship tests. On the base of the Tank he created a scientific institution (later named after him) which also included physicalchemical laboratories for research on ship construction materials, a mechanical laboratory and an electrical engineering laboratory. Krylov’s work at the Tank covered such problems as buoyancy*, stability, vibration, rolling and pitching*. In 1914 he became a member of the Russian Academy of Sciences. He was one of the first scientists to travel abroad after the October Revolution to re-establish* contacts between Russia and the West. In 1931 he found a new method of solving the equation determining the frequency of vibrations* in mechanical systems.
