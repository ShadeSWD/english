HAVE YOUR SAY, MR SILICONEСлово силикону

We all want modern materials to work better, last longer, be stronger and more reliable. Thanks to silicones this dream is now coming true. So, what are they? Silicones are synthetic polymers which combine the qualities of metals and plastics. Their molecular chains* include silicon together with carbon (C), hydrogen (H), oxygen (O), and sometimes other elements. These chains can be longer or shorter, and have different structures, so silicones can take a wide range of physical forms, from oils to resins and rubbers. Silicones are chemically inert and can resist such aggressive factors as chemicals or ultraviolet radiation. This makes them the best material for protective coatings in many areas – from historic monuments to microelectronic devices. Thin films of silicone rubber provide protection from heat and electrical insulation, which lets our gadgets become smaller, thinner, and work faster. Silicones do not change their physical properties over a much wider temperature range than other polymers, and they can do their work even in outer space.* They also have many ‘down to Earth’ applications – from wire insulation* in electrical sparking* equipment to mobile phone keypads and kitchen tools.* Silicone sealants resist water, fuels* and oils, so they are now popular both in industry and at home. In windows these sealants keep the weather out, making houses more energy-efficient. In bathrooms and kitchens they prevent leaks.* In shipbuilding, hatch seals* and cargo doors* of a ship are ideal areas for silicone. Silicone adhesives are now everywhere. A GODSENDНаходка

‘Necessity is the mother of invention*,’ people say. What is more, inventions are not usually planned, most of them come into the world just by chance. Here’s a

In the 1970s, like today, lots of people worked in offices. They didn’t have personal computers then and kept a great number of documents on their desks. It was very difficult to find things quickly and office workers wished they could have something to help them organise their files. Arthur Fry and Spencer Silver worked for an engineering company. One day, Silver got a task to develop a super-strong adhesive for use in aerospace industry. He started working, but instead of a strong adhesive he made a kind so weak that it could stick to surfaces* and then come off* easily. Nobody saw any application for it, so the adhesive was quickly forgotten. Arthur Fry sang in a church choir* on Sundays. Like all the other singers he used a hymnbook* to read the words from. But his bookmarks kept falling out* of the hymnbook and he always had a problem finding the right page. Fry needed something to stop his bookmarks from falling, and suddenly he remembered Silver’s adhesive. He applied some of it to his bookmarks and … his problem was solved! Fry began to use his invention at work. As soon as other people in the company saw the ‘sticky bookmarks’, they started coming to his office asking him for more and more. And that is how the Post-It® notes appeared. Today they come in all shapes and sizes, in over 30 different colours and you can find them everywhere: in offices and homes, on files, computers, desks and fridges throughout the world. We can almost call this invention a Godsend*, as Fry originally designed the notes for use in a hymnbook! The adhesive in Post-It® notes is silicone-based. It is made of very long chain silicone polymer and a resinous material. They let the note stick to the surface when you press it, and then come off easily when you don’t need it any more.
