GLIMPSES OF SAINT PETERSBURGШтрихи к портрету города

In 1700 Peter I started the Northern war to get back the Russian lands which were at the time under Swedish control. On gaining access to the Baltic Sea in 1703, he founded a fortress in the mouth of the Neva, and the history of Saint Petersburg began. The same year the first foreign ship called* at its port, thus recognising Russia’s new city. On the left bank of the Neva, just opposite the fortress, the Admiralty yard was founded to build ships for the Navy. The Swedes tried to destroy the yard several times, but failed. The young Russian Navy finally won the war, and the victory made the country a naval power.

In 1712, long before the war was over, Peter I moved the capital from Moscow to Saint Petersburg. The city with its many rivers and canals was planned by him as a replica* of Amsterdam – his favourite place in Europe. That is why one of the islands in the centre of Saint Petersburg is called ‘New Holland,’ and it is the place where the first test tank in Russia was opened in 1894. The experiments were supervised* by A.N.Krylov, and the tank later gave birth to the famous Krylov Research Centre.

Science became part of the city’s life from the very beginning. As early as 1724 the Academy of Sciences together with Saint Petersburg university and a gymnasium were founded by order of Peter the Great, and outstanding scientists were invited from Europe to work and teach there. M.V.Lomonosov was Head of the university from 1758 till 1765, and he was the first to give lectures in Russian. With the growth of the city the number of educational institutions also grew, and there are about 60 research centres and over 80 higher schools in Saint Petersburg now. They train students in different disciplines which are necessary for the modern economy.

Saint Petersburg is also one of the largest industrial centres in Russia. Maritime industry is traditionally a very important field here. Up to 80% of shipbuilding research institutions and about 30% of Russian shipbuilding yards are located in the area. They build all kinds of ships – tankers, icebreakers, submarines and many others (except aircraft-carriers). The city’s maritime industry is unique because the yards are provided with all the necessary equipment for the ships by the local plants.

Saint Petersburg has many power-engineering* plants which produce machinery for power stations*. The city is also a mechanical engineering* centre. Railway carriages*, cars and buses, diesel engines, and even spaceship equipment are made here. Moreover, it is famous for its electronics and instrument-making industries, whose products are exported to many countries. The innovative activity and economic efficiency of Saint Petersburg plants are well above average* in Russia due to* the high educational and cultural standards of the city’s people.

While Moscow is Russia’s political capital, Saint Petersburg is often called the ‘cultural capital.’ More than a hundred different festivals and other events take place here every year. The city has over 40 art galleries and 80 theatres, the world-famous the Mariinsky Opera House among them. There are about 50 museums in the city. Its main museum, the State Hermitage, is one of the largest in the world. It attracts visitors not only by its rich art collections, but as a residence of the Russian tsars. The first public museum in Russia, the Kunstkammer, was founded by Peter I and based on his personal collection.

However, the largest and most important museum is the city itself. Unlike many other cities, it was built according to a plan, and its architectural compositions are known all over the world. They were created by such outstanding architects as C.Rossi, B.Rastrelli, G.Quarenghi, A.Monferrand, A.D.Zakharov, A.N.Voronikhin, and many others.
