UNIVERSITY LIFEУниверситетская жизнь

The merry-go-round* of university life is something that nobody can forget. The first of September comes, and the excited* firstyear students gather in the university hall, where the rector, the deans, lecturers and teachers greet them. The monitors hand out student cards, record books and library cards – everyone feels like a ‘real’ person. But the next day the celebration is over and hard work begins. It isn’t easy to keep up with the programme, as classes and seminars follow lectures and there is so much homework to do. The autumn term comes to its end, and here is December with the test week. Everyone tries to get the necessary passes to go on and take their exams. There are successes* and failures, the first achievements and the first disappointments. The exams begin in January. ‘Oh, dear!* Why are there so many questions on the list?’ ‘Where can I find the material on this topic? There is such a short time for preparation!’ Everyone hopes to pass their exams, but some fail. Tears and smiles, and the long-awaited holiday. The merry-go-round runs faster. HIGHER EDUCATION IN RUSSIAВысшее образование в России

There are two levels of higher education in Russia today – undergraduate and postgraduate. Undergraduate studies usually last four years and the programmes include theoretical courses in sciences, humanities and social-economic disciplines, as well as professional training. After a course of full-time study, students write and defend diploma projects and take the State final examination. They receive their first university level degree, the Bachelor’s degree (B.Sc. or B.A.). Some people choose to study for the Specialist Diploma, which takes five years. It is more professionally and practically oriented than a Bachelor’s degree course, and it is also the traditional way to doctoral studies. As with the Bachelor’s degree, in their final year the students defend diploma projects and sit the State final examination. While the first two are undergraduate courses, a Master’s degree programme belongs to the postgraduate higher education. This two-year course focuses on research activities more than a Specialist’s programme does. After doing a Master’s degree, students can continue their work to get a doctoral degree: Candidate of Science / Humanities degree (equivalent to Ph.D. – Doctor of Philosophy – in the USA and Western Europe) and Doctor of Science / Humanities degree (equivalent to Professor).
